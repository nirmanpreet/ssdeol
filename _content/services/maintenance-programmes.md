## The problem with reactive painting

Reactive painting costs more than planned painting, and not only in the rate.

A surface that has been left until it "looks bad" has usually gone past the
point where a repaint is enough. Now you are paying for stripping, repairs and
remediation as well as painting — and often for out-of-hours work to fit around
a building that is still in use.

Planning the cycle means the worst intervention is designed out before it
happens.

## What a programme includes

- **A survey of every site**, so the scope is known rather than discovered
- **A multi-year schedule** by building, elevation and element
- **Phasing that follows your calendar** — quiet periods, shutdowns, audit dates
- **Budgeting by year**, so finance can plan rather than absorb
- **Consistent specification**, so every building matches
- **Annual reporting** — what was done, what is coming next year, what needs
  attention sooner

## Where the value actually is

1. **Cost predictability.** A known annual number instead of a surprise.
2. **Lower cost per job.** Specified work in a quiet period beats an emergency
   call-out.
3. **No accumulation of deferred problems.** Because the schedule is visible,
   nothing quietly gets worse for three years.
4. **Consistency across the portfolio.** One crew, one specification, one
   standard — every site.

## Getting started

Most programmes begin with a single site and a first full cycle. Once you have
seen the reporting and the quality in practice, expanding to more sites is
straightforward. We will scope and price the first cycle with no obligation to
continue.
