## When rope access is the right call

Rope access makes sense when:

- The building is too tall for a boom lift
- The footprint will not take a scaffold without closing the site
- The elevation is complex or curved, where scaffold would need rebuilding
- The work is short and specific, where the cost of erecting and striking
  scaffold is not justified
- Access has to be maintained underneath — which rope access does, since only a
  small area is restricted at a time

It is **not** automatically the cheaper option. Scaffold is usually faster and
cheaper on a long, straight elevation. We will price both and explain which is
right for your building, rather than default to whichever suits us.

## What we do before anyone leaves the ground

- **Site-specific Safe Work Method Statement.** Work over two metres triggers
  this, and most commercial work does.
- **Risk assessment** for the specific elevation, including the ground below,
  public access, and what happens if a tool drops.
- **Rescue plan.** Who rescues whom, how, and how fast. Every rope access job
  needs a written one.
- **Competency and equipment records** available on request.
- **Equipment inspection** before every use.

## Public buildings and occupied sites

Where the public is beneath or adjacent, the work is planned differently:
dropped-object protection, exclusion zones, and often agreed hours. If that
applies to your site, say so when you enquire and we will factor it in.
