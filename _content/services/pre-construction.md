  - q: What is two-pack finishing and when is it needed?
    a:
      - Two-pack is a chemically cured finish — a resin and a hardener that react to form a very tough coating.

## What we do on a new build

- **Interior fit-off** — walls, ceilings, joinery, architraves, skirtings
- **External coats** — panel walls, cladding, steelwork, canopies, roller doors
- **Two-pack finishes** — kitchens, bathrooms, laboratories, food preparation,
  medical rooms and any surface that gets washed and scrubbed
- **Touch-up and pre-handover** — final coat after all other trades have finished
  and made their mess

## Working as a subcontractor

We work under head contractors regularly, which means we understand what is
actually required of a trade on a live site:

- We quote from the scope and drawings, and we **query gaps in writing** rather
  than guessing and pricing the risk into the number
- We work to the **builder's programme**, not ours
- We report to the site manager, attend toolbox talks, and follow the site's
  safety system
- We **do not** claim variations we have not had confirmed

## Programme, honestly

Painting sits near the end of a fit-off, which makes it a common source of
programme pressure. Where dates are tight we will say so at quote stage rather
than accept a date we know we will miss. The cheapest way to protect a
programme is to be straight about it at the start.

## Product and warranty

If we supply the coating system, the warranty sits with us and covers the system
we specified. If you supply the paint, we apply it as directed and the warranty
runs with that product. Both are legitimate; it just needs to be clear whose
warranty you hold before you appoint anyone.
