## What we mark

- **Aisle and traffic lanes** — boundaries between pedestrians and vehicles
- **Pedestrian walkways** — protected routes, ideally physically separated where
  the layout allows
- **Hazard zones** — battery charging areas, chemical stores, gas cylinder
  points, loading edges
- **Storage bay and racking markings** — racking uprights, floor-level stock
  positions, clear aisle widths
- **Fire and emergency equipment zones** — kept clear of stock, with the correct
  markings
- **Vehicle and equipment parking** — consistent positions that people
  actually use
- **Pedestrian crossings** — where people and vehicles must cross

## Before we paint anything

Line marking looks simple and is easy to get wrong. The marking itself is the
cheap part; deciding where the lines go is the work.

We will look at:

- **Actual traffic flow**, not just the floor plan. Where do people walk
  currently? Where do they cut corners?
- **The forklift turning radius** and the clear aisle width your racking
  requires.
- **Existing concrete joints and cracks** — lines laid across a moving joint will
  crack.
- **Surface condition.** Marking on a floor that is about to be coated is
  wasted money.

## Marking on coated floors

If you are installing epoxy or polyurea, get the marking done as part of the
same job. Lines applied *after* coating sit on top of it and can be scuffed or
picked up by pallet trucks. Lines applied *into* the coating during application
become part of the floor and wear at the same rate as everything around them.

If your floor is already coated and the lines are gone, we can re-mark the
existing coating — but we will tell you whether a full recoat would be better
value than re-marking a floor that is nearly due anyway.
