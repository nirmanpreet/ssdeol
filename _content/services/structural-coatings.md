## Structural steel

Steelwork fails by corroding, and corrosion rarely announces itself — it starts
as surface rust, becomes scale, and eventually eats section thickness away.
Coating is the control, and the control only works if the preparation is right.

**What we do:**

- **Assess** the condition — rust grade, scale, pitting, remaining section
- **Prepare** by abrasive blasting or hand and power tool cleaning to the
  standard the coating system requires
- **Coat** with a primer, intermediate and finish coat system specified for the
  exposure and the environment
- **Document** the system, the products, the dry film thickness achieved and the
  date, for your building file

## Tilt-up panel exteriors

Tilt-up is the default construction for warehouses and industrial buildings
across most of Victoria, and it has three recurring problems:

1. **Failed panel joint sealant.** The single biggest cause of water ingress and
   the first thing we check.
2. **Rust staining from fixings** and embeds.
3. **Coating breakdown** from UV exposure and chalking, usually worst on the
   sunny elevations.

We seal, repair and coat the whole elevation so it is one consistent system
rather than three trades visiting separately.

## Fire-rating and intumescent coatings

Where steel is required to have a fire resistance, an intumescent coating forms
part of that assembly. This is a specified, documented system — product, dry
film thickness, surface preparation standard and application records.

**We will give you the paperwork.** Application records and product data matter
when a building is sold, insured or audited.

## Environment matters

Coating specification is not a one-size answer. The same steel in Docklands, in
Geelong's industrial estate and in regional Victoria is in three different
environments, and the correct system changes with it. Salt-laden coastal air is
the most aggressive; a sheltered inland shed is close to mild.
