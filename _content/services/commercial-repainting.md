## What a commercial repaint actually involves

A repaint is only as good as what happens before the first coat goes on.
Overpainting a dirty, chalky or rusting surface is the single most common reason
a commercial paint job fails early, and it is almost always avoidable.

So the order we work in is fixed:

1. **Inspect and identify.** What is on the surface now, what is underneath it,
   and is anything failing?
2. **Repair.** Cracks, failed joints, rust, water-damaged board, perished
   sealant. Skipped, a new coating fails with the old one.
3. **Prepare.** Wash down, scrape, sand, and strip coatings that have lost
   adhesion. This is the least glamorous part of the job and the most important.
4. **Prime.** The right primer for the substrate and the topcoat — not one primer
   for everything.
5. **Coat.** Two coats minimum, laid off and rolled properly, not sprayed thin.
6. **Inspect and hand over.** We walk it with you before we call it finished.

## Interior commercial painting

Offices, showrooms, staff areas, amenities, breakout spaces, stairwells and
corridors. We can work to your corporate palette, match an existing colour for
patch repairs, and sequence the job so one part of the floor always works
normally.

We can also run this as a **phased programme** — a set number of areas each
week, scheduled around your financial year and any audit dates you are working
back to.

## Exterior commercial and industrial painting

Tilt-up panel walls, concrete panels, metal cladding, roller doors, canopies and
exposed structure. Exterior work is where access, weather and height turn a
straightforward repaint into a planning exercise, so we talk about all three
before we quote:

- **Access.** Scaffolding, boom lift or rope access. Each has a different cost
  and a different effect on how long the job takes.
- **Height.** Anything over two metres triggers a Safe Work Method Statement.
  We prepare one for every job.
- **Weather windows.** External coatings need the right temperature and
  dryness. We will tell you if your programme is unrealistic rather than find
  out halfway through.

## Working around your operation

This is the part we are asked about most, so we ask it first. Depending on the
site we can:

- work nights, weekends and public holidays
- work only inside your shutdown window
- move zone by zone so the building stays partly open
- isolate and protect plant, and work around it rather than through it
- keep fire exits, aisles and access routes clear and usable throughout

## Preparation — the bit that actually protects your coating

| Surface | What we check |
|---|---|
| Concrete panels | Cracking, carbonation, spalling, moisture at joints |
| Tilt-up panels | Joint sealant condition, panel movement, rust staining from fixings |
| Metal cladding | Corrosion, fixing failure, coating breakdown at seams |
| Internal plasterboard | Existing paint adhesion, water staining, joint cracking |
| Brickwork | Efflorescence, spalling, mortar condition |
| Steel | Surface rust, scale, weld grinding, whether it needs rust removal first |

If something on this list needs more than preparation, we will say so in the
quote. It is not a surprise you want on day one of a shutdown.
