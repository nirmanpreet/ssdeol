  - q: Is epoxy or polyurea better for our floor?
    a:
      - Epoxy is the workhorse. It is tough, economical, and available in a wide range of colours and finishes. For most warehouses and factories it is the right answer.

      - A single-coat epoxy system over prepared concrete typically needs overnight to a day or two. Polyurea cures much faster and can be walked on sooner.

  - q: Can you do the floor in stages?
    a:
      - Yes, and we usually do. Warehouse floors can be divided by bay or by aisle so you keep using part of the building.

    a:
      - A smooth epoxy floor is considerably more slip-resistant than bare concrete, but it is not the same as a dedicated anti-slip system.

## Why ordinary floor paint fails

Warehouse concrete is a hard, porous, chemically active surface. Standard floor
paint sits on top of it rather than bonding to it, wears through in the traffic
lanes, and starts flaking at the edges of the worn patch — which then takes the
coating further away with it.

An industrial coating system bonds or chemically locks into the substrate,
carries the load across the surface rather than at a point, and can be built up
to a thickness that resists forklift traffic and pallet drag.

## The systems we install

| System | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Epoxy primer + epoxy topcoat** | Most warehouses, factories, workshops | The default. Tough, economical, wide colour range. |
| **Epoxy with anti-slip aggregate** | Wet areas, ramps, kitchens, walkways | Slip resistance at the cost of a slightly harder clean |
| **Polyurea** | Cold rooms, food processing, heavy thermal cycling | Cures fast, handles chemical and thermal shock |
| **Polyurethane / urethane** | Areas needing UV stability and a harder finish | Thinner build, harder surface |
| **Tinted sealer** | Budget refresh where full removal is not required | Shortened life. We will say when this is and is not enough. |

The specification is driven by three things: **what the forklift traffic
actually is**, **what gets spilled on it**, and **how hot and wet it gets**.

## Preparation is the job

The coating is the cheap part. Getting the floor ready is what determines
whether it lasts five years or one.

Our preparation usually involves:

- **Grinding** to open the pores of the concrete and remove the old coating.
  This removes the need for chemical stripping and produces a dust-controlled
  surface.
- **Crack and joint repair**, filled and blended so the coating can run
  continuous across them.
- **Repair of damaged or spalled concrete** before any coating goes down.
- **Moisture testing** — a coating applied over a wet slab will lift, and testing
  costs very little compared with redoing a floor.
- **Dust extraction and containment** so we are not coating your stock instead
  of your floor.

## Working inside a live site

Floor work is the hardest thing to do around an operating building, so it is
where planning matters most:

- **Over a shutdown.** One area at a time, so you still have somewhere to
  operate from.
- **Phased by bay or aisle.** Planned joins, so the floor is continuous in the
  end.
- **Nights and weekends.** Grinding, coating and curing overnight where the
  traffic allows.
- **Heavy-duty build** for high-traffic lanes, with a lighter system in the
  low-traffic areas so you are not paying for performance you do not need.
